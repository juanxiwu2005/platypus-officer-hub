# Cheers points system: research summary (2026-11-26)

Juanxi asked for research on the purpose, make-up, rewards and reset period of the Cheers points system, with attention to student clubs and nonprofits that have little money, and how clubs of different sizes can use the same points with different prizes. Three research agents covered (1) academic evidence, (2) how recognition products design points, (3) low-cost rewards, ASU funding rules, scaling and resets. This file records the findings and the open decisions; the Chinese review page for Juanxi is linked in `docs/PROGRESS.md`. Nothing here changes product behavior.

## 1. Evidence (academic)

- Specific written thanks works: positive feedback raises intrinsic motivation (d ≈ +0.33; Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, 128 studies); a director's thank-you raised fundraisers' calls about 50% the next week (Grant & Gino 2010); unannounced thank-you cards raised later output, mostly through people who did not get one (Bradler et al. 2016); a no-cash "best performer" card raised performance about 12% (Kosfeld & Neckermann 2011).
- Symbolic awards keep newcomers in volunteer communities (Gallus 2017, Wikipedia field experiment, effect lasting four quarters); discretionary awards for exceptional service beat automatic ones (Walk, Zhang & Littlepage 2019, Boy Scouts data).
- Money framing and small expected rewards can backfire: small pay lowered donations collected (Gneezy & Rustichini 2000); a €7 gift worked where €7 cash did not (Kube, Maréchal & Puppe 2012); a perfect-attendance gift-card raffle lowered plant productivity 1.4% and alienated people who were already punctual (Gubler, Larkin & Pierce 2016).
- Points raise quantity, not quality (Mekler et al. 2017; Cerasoli, Nicklin & Ford 2014). Leaderboards and badges lowered motivation over a semester (Hanus & Fox 2015); middle ranks lower effort by more than 10% (Gill et al. 2019); disclosing rank hurt low performers (Ashraf, Bandiera & Lee 2014).
- Peer recognition specifically is thin: at NASA, manager recognition raised engagement, peer recognition had no overall effect but helped less-visible staff (Gallus, Jung & Lakhani, working paper). No causal study of peer-bonus allowances (Google, Bonusly) was found.
- Streaks and expiry pressure distort behavior (GitHub streak removal, Moldon et al. 2021); point currencies inflate or are gamed through reciprocity without caps (Kash, Friedman & Halpern; Livan et al. 2017).
- Reset period: no experiment compares weekly, monthly or semester resets. The closest evidence is the fresh-start effect after landmarks such as a new semester (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014).

## 2. Products

- Points per dollar, where stated: Bonusly, Nectar and Matter 10 points = $1; Workhuman 20 = $1; HeyTaco suggests 4 tacos = $1. 100 points = $1 appears only as a Workhuman blog example. Typical giving allowances are worth about $3–10 per person per month (Bonusly 100 points a month; Matter 80 a week; Motivosity recommends $3–5 a month).
- Universal pattern: two balances. Points to give come from the organization and expire each period; points received belong to the person and usually never expire.
- Several products give points no money value (Kudos, Cooleaf) and allow zero-point recognition (Bonusly, Nectar). Low-cost "custom rewards" fulfilled by the organization are common: lunch with a leader, choosing the team lunch, leading a meeting, a day off, swag.
- Most are public by default; visibility is the main defense against collusion. Hard rules: no self-giving, no transfer of allowance, cost multiplies per recipient, approval for large awards.
- Campus tools (Anthology Engage, Presence, CampusGroups) and Greek-life standards use fixed thresholds ("150 points: swag; 500: VIP access; 750: banquet recognition"), not stores.

## 3. Low-cost rewards and ASU rules

- What volunteers value most is personal, specific thanks through the year and recognition of skills useful for school and careers (Volunteer Canada 2013; Points of Light; VolunteerHub practice guidance).
- USG Tempe funding rules ("Prohibited Expenses", asuusgt.org/funding-rules) list "'Gifts', 'Prizes', 'Giveaways' … Gift certificates are prohibited" and "Awards, trophies, certificates, and plaques for membership recognition". So officer-recognition prizes cannot use USG money. ASU treats gift cards as cash equivalents (older EOSS gift card policy); IRS Publication 15-B says gift cards are never de minimis. Student organizations generally cannot run raffles (ASU Office of General Counsel). The research could not verify the exact wording "reimbursements cannot be paid by gift card" on the current USG site; earlier repository text citing the policy archive (ASU-R-037/R-038) should be checked against the archive before it is quoted again.
- ASU issues digital credentials for club membership and leadership (ASU Student Life); whether a club can issue them itself is not verified.
